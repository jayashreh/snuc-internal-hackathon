### Section 1: Right Triangle Definitions (SOH CAH TOA)
Trigonometric functions for an acute angle θ in a right triangle are defined as ratios of the side lengths.
Sine (sin) = Opposite / Hypotenuse
Cosine (cos) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
Tangent (tan) = Opposite / Adjacent
The mnemonic SOH CAH TOA helps remember these fundamental ratios. The other three functions are reciprocals of these.

### Section 2: Reciprocal and Quotient Identities
The six trigonometric functions relate to each other through fundamental reciprocal and quotient identities. Whenever you are stuck, converting all functions to sine and cosine is a powerful first step.
Reciprocal Identities:
csc(θ) = 1 / sin(θ)
sec(θ) = 1 / cos(θ)
cot(θ) = 1 / tan(θ)
Quotient Identities:
tan(θ) = sin(θ) / cos(θ)
cot(θ) = cos(θ) / sin(θ)

### Section 3: Pythagorean Identities
The Pythagorean identities are based on the Pythagorean theorem applied to the unit circle. Look for squared trigonometric terms to apply these.
1. Primary Identity: sin²(θ) + cos²(θ) = 1
2. Tangent/Secant Identity: 1 + tan²(θ) = sec²(θ) (Derived by dividing the primary identity by cos²(θ))
3. Cotangent/Cosecant Identity: 1 + cot²(θ) = csc²(θ) (Derived by dividing the primary identity by sin²(θ))
These can be rearranged. For example, sin²(θ) = 1 - cos²(θ).

### Section 4: Even-Odd (Negative Angle) Identities
These identities show how trigonometric functions behave with negative angles, based on their symmetry on the graph.
Cosine and Secant are "even" functions, meaning the negative sign disappears:
cos(-θ) = cos(θ)
sec(-θ) = sec(θ)
Sine, Cosecant, Tangent, and Cotangent are "odd" functions, meaning the negative sign moves to the front:
sin(-θ) = -sin(θ)
csc(-θ) = -csc(θ)
tan(-θ) = -tan(θ)
cot(-θ) = -cot(θ)

### Section 5: Co-Function Identities
Co-function identities relate the trigonometric functions of complementary angles (angles that add up to 90 degrees or π/2 radians).
sin(π/2 - θ) = cos(θ)
cos(π/2 - θ) = sin(θ)
tan(π/2 - θ) = cot(θ)
csc(π/2 - θ) = sec(θ)
sec(π/2 - θ) = csc(θ)
cot(π/2 - θ) = tan(θ)

### Section 6: Double Angle Formulas
Double angle formulas are used when the angle inside the trigonometric function is multiplied by two.
Sine Double Angle: sin(2θ) = 2 * sin(θ) * cos(θ)
Cosine Double Angle: 
cos(2θ) = cos²(θ) - sin²(θ)
cos(2θ) = 2cos²(θ) - 1
cos(2θ) = 1 - 2sin²(θ)
Tangent Double Angle: tan(2θ) = (2 * tan(θ)) / (1 - tan²(θ))

### Section 7: Problem-Solving Strategy for Verifying Identities
When verifying complex trigonometric identities or solving equations:
Step 1: Work on the more complicated side of the equation first.
Step 2: Look for opportunities to factor expressions, add fractions, or square a binomial.
Step 3: Look for opportunities to use the Pythagorean identities (especially if you see terms with an exponent of 2).
Step 4: When in doubt, convert every single term to sines and cosines using reciprocal and quotient identities.
Step 5: Never cross the equals sign; simplify one side until it completely matches the other.
